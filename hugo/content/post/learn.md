What does it mean to [learn][learn] (see also [Learning][learning])? It's a popular word. If "some
machines" can learn (see [Machine Learning][ml]) should we learn to think like them, or they like
us?

# Learn from data

[lfd]: {{< relref "learn-from-data.md" >}}

Is it redundant to say we [Learn from data][lfd]? If you take "data" to mean anything from a web
article to a database table, it seems so. Still, can an idea that appears in a dream teach you
something, or data generated by a simulation?

If you take "experience" as a synonym for "data" then this definition of machine learning implies
"learn from data" is redundant:
> A computer program is said to learn from experience E with respect to some class of tasks T and
> performance measure P if its performance at tasks in T, as measured by P, improves with experience
> E.

# Learn for credit

[br]: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basic_research
[ci]: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Causal_inference
[lfc]: {{< relref "learn-for-credit.md" >}}
[why]: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Why

Similarly, is it redundant to say we [Learn for credit][lfc]? Does "pure" research ever make sense,
or should we call it [Basic research][br]? That is, should you ever learn or develop a solution
without a problem? Does four years of college without an application in mind make sense? Should you
be thinking about your life goals with every step you take? Should you let yourself "learn" values
(see [Learning][learning])?

The word [Why][why] is used (confusingly) in both [Causal inference][ci] and to query the value
behind an action. Does a toaster warm up bread because it's hot or because that makes it delicious?
